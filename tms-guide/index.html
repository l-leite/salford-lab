Two modes.
One meaningful
place.

Combine two familiar spaces. Find what connects their activities. Use that relationship to invent a hybrid archetype: a starting idea for your architecture.

15 possibilities to explore. You can adapt one, or invent your own.

THINK

Read, research, reflect, imagine, discuss, listen.

MAKE

Craft, repair, grow, cook, print, prototype.

SHOW

Exhibit, demonstrate, exchange, teach, broadcast.

01 /

Build your idea in four steps.

STEP 01

Choose two modes.

Which activities do you want to bring together?

THINK + MAKE
MAKE + SHOW
THINK + SHOW

STEP 02

Find two spaces.

Choose one familiar spatial type for each mode. Think about who comes and what they do.

For example: reading room + print workshop.

STEP 03

Find the connection.

Why do they belong together? Can one activity feed, support or challenge the other?

Try a sequence, contrast, exchange, view, transformation or shared activity.

STEP 04

Name it. Sketch it.

Describe the shared purpose in a sentence. Draw the relationship and ask one architectural question.

Explore the connection before committing to a floor plan.

15 ways to begin.

Each example combines two familiar spaces with a shared purpose. The sketch prompts are possibilities to test. These are starting points, not prescribed buildings or room lists.

01THINK + MAKE

The Slow Commons

Café + Study Room

Food is prepared and shared while students, researchers and neighbours read, work and exchange ideas around a common table.

Try in a sketch

Place a shared eating-and-working table between food preparation and quieter study.

How can sociable cooking support quiet concentration?

02THINK + MAKE

The Listening Forge

Instrument Workshop + Contemplative Room

Makers craft and repair instruments, then listen closely to their sound; what they hear guides the next adjustment.

Try in a sketch

Connect a workbench to a listening room through an acoustic buffer.

How do you move from the noise of making to careful listening?

06THINK + MAKE

The Paper House

Reading Room + Print Workshop

Readers, writers and illustrators turn researched ideas into books, posters and small publications, then return to discuss them.

Try in a sketch

Draw a route from reading to editing, printing and binding, with a shared review table.

Where does a private idea become something tangible and shared?

07THINK + MAKE

The Mending Library

Library + Repair Workshop

Neighbours learn how everyday objects work and use that knowledge to repair them together, rather than replace them.

Try in a sketch

Put a diagnostic table between reference shelves and a shared repair bench.

How can learning and repairing happen side by side?

08THINK + MAKE

The Clay Cloister

Ceramics Atelier + Contemplative Courtyard

People shape clay, pause to observe light and texture in a quiet courtyard, then return to making with fresh ideas.

Try in a sketch

Arrange working edges and places to pause around a courtyard; test the threshold between them.

How can a pause in making become part of the creative process?

03MAKE + SHOW

The Repair Salon

Garage + Showroom

Enthusiasts repair, restore or customise bicycles, vehicles or mechanical objects, then share the work and its story with visitors.

Try in a sketch

Give visitors a view into a repair bay and trace a separate route for moving objects.

How can repair become visible while work and visitor routes stay safe?

04MAKE + SHOW

The Growing Exchange

Greenhouse + Marketplace

Growers cultivate plants and food, then exchange produce, seeds and knowledge with neighbours who can see where it all begins.

Try in a sketch

Link growing beds to an exchange table, allowing space for harvesting and washing along the way.

How can a journey from growing to exchange make production understandable?

09MAKE + SHOW

The Stitch Exchange

Textile Workshop + Market Arcade

People make, alter and mend textiles, then display, exchange or sell them along a public edge where neighbours can join in.

Try in a sketch

Let a workshop open onto a sheltered arcade through a demonstration or mending table.

Where can a passer-by become a participant?

10MAKE + SHOW

The Play Foundry

Toy Workshop + Playground

Makers build toys and games that others try through play; watching how they are used helps improve the next version.

Try in a sketch

Connect a protected making area to a supervised play space through a testing threshold.

How can play become feedback for making?

11MAKE + SHOW

The Light Foundry

Fabrication Workshop + Light Chamber

People make lamps and light installations, then invite visitors to experience and discuss their effects on shadow and atmosphere.

Try in a sketch

Draw a transition from a well-lit workbench to a chamber with controlled light.

How does the journey from making to darkness prepare someone to see?

The Idea Assembly

Reading Room + Public Forum

People research and develop ideas, then share and challenge them through informal talks and small public discussions.

Try in a sketch

Connect quiet reading places to a modest discussion space through a shared gathering edge.

How can someone move between private reflection and public debate?

12THINK + SHOW

The Atlas Room

Research Library + Public Mapping Room

Researchers and neighbours investigate the city, share their findings through maps and invite others to add local knowledge.

Try in a sketch

Extend a research table into a public mapping surface where visitors can contribute.

How can private research become knowledge that others can question?

13THINK + SHOW

The Signal House

Research Studio + Broadcasting Room

People investigate contemporary questions and share their findings through podcasts, radio conversations and small public broadcasts.

Try in a sketch

Link a research table to an acoustically protected broadcast room with a visible public edge.

How can a quiet recording space connect with an audience?

14THINK + SHOW

The Climate Lantern

Observation Laboratory + Public Display

People study local weather, light and temperature, then make their observations visible so neighbours can understand changing conditions.

Try in a sketch

Link an observation point to a public wall or light display that responds to its findings.

How can a building help people notice an environmental change?

15THINK + SHOW

The Night Observatory

Study Room + Sky Terrace

People study the sky and celestial movements, then gather on a shared terrace to observe, explain and discuss their discoveries.

Try in a sketch

Draw an accessible journey from a study table to an open sky view, with a dimly lit transition.

How can a shared place reveal the night sky while limiting light spill?

03 /

Now make the idea yours.

The [name] is a place where [people] come to [activity A] and [activity B], connected through [shared purpose].

Bring three things to the table:

  1. One sentence explaining who it is for and why the activities belong together.
  2. One relationship diagram showing activities, connections and any conflict to resolve.
  3. One architectural question about light, sound, access, visibility, thresholds or atmosphere.
500–750 m² GFA

Work towards a modest building, usually one or two storeys: one substantial shared space, a complementary space and a few supporting functions. Test the scale, servicing and access of your chosen activities. Start with the relationship; develop the plan afterwards.
