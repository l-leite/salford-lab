The brief asks you to design a space for work. A mode of work describes a way of working: THINK, MAKE or SHOW. Choose two modes to guide your proposal.

Sixteen spatial strategies. Twenty-four examples. A starting point for your own proposal.

What is a mode of work?

A mode is a way of working. The term supports this studio brief: THINK, MAKE and SHOW each include many processes and can share a space. Choose a pair of modes, then define who uses the workspace and what their work needs.

Develop your proposal in four steps.

Choose two modes.

STEP 02

Define the work.

Identify credible users and the processes each mode supports. Ask what spatial conditions they need.

For THINK + MAKE: researching, writing, printing and binding.

STEP 03

Test arrangements.

Explore how the modes relate: nested, adjacent, overlapping, separated or fully combined.

A hybrid can occupy one space, several spaces or related buildings.

STEP 04

State it. Sketch it.

Explain the purpose of your pair of modes. Compare spatial diagrams and ask an architectural question.

Test light, sound, access, structure and atmosphere before fixing a plan.

04 /

Develop your own pair of modes.

The [workspace] supports the modes [mode A] and [mode B] for [people]. Their work involves [specific processes], arranged as [spatial relationship] to support [purpose].

Bring three things for discussion:

  1. One sentence naming your pair of modes, the users and the purpose of their work.
  2. Two or three spatial diagrams comparing arrangements. Use the mode colours, label each mode and identify conflicts to resolve.
  3. One architectural question about light, sound, access, structure, material or atmosphere, linked to your preferred arrangement.
Approx. 750 m² GIA

The brief gives an indicative gross internal area of approximately 750 m². Keep the programme credible for your users and pair of modes; justify significant departures. Test scale, servicing and access as you develop the spatial arrangement and its response to the site.
