§01

The marking structure

The portfolio is assessed through four weighted criteria. The same drawing or model can provide evidence for more than one criterion.

Assessment weights

Guide commentary

Develop all four criteria. Make the links between research, testing, decisions and the final proposal clear.

§02

The four criteria

Official wording appears in plain panels. Shaded panels contain guide commentary and examples.

AC1

Site Engagement and Analysis

20%

Official wording

Analysing the urban elements and factors that generate the design potentials and limitations. The design responds to the site's built urban context, including its history, movement networks, and relationship to the park and footbridge. The architectural and environmental response reflects an understanding of the site's physical, social, and spatial characteristics.

Learning outcomes: LO1 · LO2 · LO5

Level 5 skills: KU1 · KU2 · RS1 · RS2 · TS1 · CS1 · CS2

Guide commentary

Show how site findings inform the design: access, movement, context and environmental conditions.

  • Link each key site observation to a decision in plan or section.
  • Explain the relationship to the park, footbridge and surrounding streets.

Avoid: Maps that describe the site without informing the proposal.

AC2

Design development and documentation

30%

Official wording

Documenting the design process through making, iteration, and reflection. Work shows how ideas evolve through sketches, models, drawings, and research. Design decisions, changes, and failures, are clearly communicated. Precedents and references support the development of programme, spatial organisationv, and atmosphere. Focusing on making connections and associations rather than collating raw data.

Learning outcomes: LO1 · LO2 · LO3 · LO4

Level 5 skills: RS1 · RS2 · PS1 · PS2 · PS3 · KU1 · KU2 · CS1 · CS2

Guide commentary

You can use the Process Journal InDesign template to document sketches, models, research, feedback and decisions. Select the clearest evidence for your portfolio.

  • Compare alternatives and explain what changed and why.
  • Show how a precedent or test informed the next design iteration.

Avoid: A sequence of images without reflection or reasons for decisions.

AC3

Narrative communication

30%

Official wording

Presenting design intent, conceptual clarity, spatial organisation, and engagement with site context. Producing clear labelled drawings (plans, sections, elevations) and visual material, including renderings, collages, physical models, diagrams, and maps, using appropriate tools and professional standards. Communicating design intentions through verbal presentation, critical thinking, and quality of argument.

Learning outcomes: LO1 · LO5

Level 5 skills: CP1 · CP2 · TS1 · TS2 · TS3

Guide commentary

Use coordinated drawings and short explanations to make the proposal easy to understand.

  • Check labels, scale, context, occupation and consistency across drawings.
  • Keep the full process record in the Process Journal InDesign template; curate the portfolio to explain the project.

Avoid: Polished visuals that leave the spatial organisation unclear.

AC4

Design resolution

20%

Official wording

The conceptual development of the building, layout, and location within the network is consistent with and aligned with the brief. Demonstrating an understanding of environmental sustainability, passive and active design strategies, grids, structural integration, and conceptual maturity. The building evokes a sense of place and atmosphere.

Learning outcomes: LO3 · LO4

Level 5 skills: TS1 · KU1 · PS1 · PS2 · PS3 · TS2 · CS1 · CS2

Guide commentary

Show how space, structure, materials and environmental strategies support the same architectural concept.

  • Coordinate plans and sections with the structural and environmental approach.
  • Explain how light, materials and spatial proportions create atmosphere.

Avoid: Technical claims or sustainability symbols unsupported by the design.

§03

Grades & full rubric

Compare the quality of your evidence with the descriptors below.

Guide commentary · grade bands

80-100 Exceptional

Rigorous enquiry, critical judgement and thorough integration across the proposal.

70-79 Very Good

Research and iteration inform decisions; communication is clear and architectural choices are effectively integrated.

60-69 Good

A coherent design process, effective communication and a well-resolved response to the brief.

50-59 Sound

Core work is present; develop iteration, clarity and integration more consistently.

40-49 Satisfactory

Basic evidence is present; strengthen its depth, completeness and coordination.

0-39 Needs Significant Improvement

Address missing or insufficient evidence with your tutor, across all four criteria.

§04

Learning outcomes

These are the five learning outcomes and their links to the assessment criteria and ARB General Criteria.

LO1

Official wording

Develop multiple design scenarios and concepts responding to the site analysis and users' needs producing a design brief to express an integrated design approach's theoretical, programmatic, and practical concerns.

Guide commentary

Compare design responses to the site and users; develop a clear brief and concept.

Assessed through: AC1 · AC2 · AC3
ARB criteria: GC1 · GC7 · GC10

LO2

Official wording

Develop a systematic understanding of the key aspects of sustainable design and construction, and their social, environmental and economic impact, in line with commonly used evaluation tools and with the international and national agendas.

Guide commentary

Explain sustainable design principles and their social, environmental and economic effects.

Assessed through: AC1 · AC2
ARB criteria: GC5 · GC9

LO3

Official wording

Apply architectural tectonics, including spatial, structural, and material strategies, to generate a feasible integrated design.

Guide commentary

Integrate spatial, structural and material choices into a feasible design.

Assessed through: AC2 · AC4
ARB criteria: GC1 · GC10

LO4

Official wording

Foster a methodical understanding of sustainable design and construction measures and evaluation tools, demonstrating the resulting environmental, social, and economic impact consistent with the relevant policies.

Guide commentary

Apply and evaluate sustainable measures; show their effects.

Assessed through: AC2 · AC4
ARB criteria: GC5 · GC9 · GC11

LO5

Official wording

Communication: Engage in collaborative practices and use digital and analogue media to generate legible, expressive, and communicative narratives for specialist and non-specialist audiences.

Guide commentary

Communicate clearly through collaboration, drawings, models and other appropriate media.

Assessed through: AC1 · AC3
ARB criteria: GC1

§05

ARB & Level 5 skills

ARB General Criteria describe professional learning. FHEQ Level 5 skills describe academic and practical capabilities. These codes have no separate percentage weighting.

ARB General Criteria

The module is mapped to GC1, GC5, GC7, GC9, GC10 and GC11.

Official wording

GC1 Ability to create architectural designs that satisfy both aesthetic and technical requirements.

Learning outcomes: LO1 · LO3 · LO5

Full criteria

The graduate will have the ability to:

GC1.1
Prepare and present building design projects of diverse scale, complexity, and type in a variety of contexts, using a range of media, and in response to a brief.
GC1.2
Understand the constructional and structural systems, the environmental strategies and the regulatory requirements that apply to the design and construction of a comprehensive design project.
GC1.3
Develop a conceptual and critical approach to architectural design that integrates and satisfies the aesthetic aspects of a building and the technical requirements of its construction and the needs of the user.

Official wording

GC5 Understanding of the relationship between people and buildings, and between buildings and their environment, and the need to relate buildings and the spaces between them to human needs and scale.

Learning outcomes: LO2 · LO4

Full criteria

The graduate will have an understanding of:

GC5.1
The needs and aspirations of building users.
GC5.2
The impact of buildings on the environment, and the precepts of sustainable design.
GC5.3
The way in which buildings fit into their local context.

Official wording

GC7 Understanding of the methods of investigation and preparation of the brief for a design project.

Learning outcomes: LO1

Full criteria

The graduate will have an understanding of:

GC7.1
The need to critically review precedents relevant to the function, organisation and technological strategy of design proposals.
GC7.2
The need to appraise and prepare building briefs of diverse scales and types, to define client and user requirements and their appropriateness to site and context.
GC7.3
The contributions of architects and co-professionals to the formulation of the brief, and the methods of investigation used in its preparation.

Official wording

GC9 Adequate knowledge of physical problems and technologies and the function of buildings so as to provide them with internal conditions of comfort and protection against the climate.

Learning outcomes: LO2 · LO4

Full criteria

The graduate will have knowledge of:

GC9.1
Principles associated with designing optimum visual, thermal and acoustic environments.
GC9.2
Systems for environmental comfort realised within relevant precepts of sustainable design.
GC9.3
Strategies for building services, and ability to integrate these in a design project.

Official wording

GC10 The necessary design skills to meet building users' requirements within the constraints imposed by cost factors and building regulations.

Learning outcomes: LO1 · LO3

Full criteria

The graduate will have the skills to:

GC10.1
Critically examine the financial factors implied in varying building types, constructional systems, and specification choices, and the impact of these on architectural design.
GC10.2
Understand the cost control mechanisms which operate during the development of a project.
GC10.3
Prepare designs that will meet building users' requirements and comply with UK legislation, appropriate performance standards and health and safety requirements.

Official wording

GC11 Adequate knowledge of the industries, organisations, regulations and procedures involved in translating design concepts into buildings and integrating plans into overall planning.

Learning outcomes: LO4

Full criteria

The graduate will have knowledge of:

GC11.1
The fundamental legal, professional and statutory responsibilities of the architect, and the organisations, regulations and procedures involved in the negotiation and approval of architectural designs, including land law, development control, building regulations and health and safety legislation.
GC11.2
The professional inter-relationships of individuals and organisations involved in procuring and delivering architectural projects, and how these are defined through contractual and organisational structures.
GC11.3
The basic management theories and business principles related to running both an architect's practice and architectural projects, recognising current and emerging trends in the construction industry.

Module aims

Official wording

A1
To enhance architectural design competencies in exploring relations between space, structure, performance, technology, environment and people.
A2
To apply theories of architecture and knowledge of the different approaches that relate to architectural design.
A3
To develop and apply methods of investigation while developing a brief for an architectural design project.
A4
To develop skills for effective communication of design ideas and information, and expressing design challenges and solutions to specialist audiences (such as engineers, managers) and to non-specialist audiences (client, end users).
